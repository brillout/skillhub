TLDR

An ADR, or architecture decision record, is a short document that captures one design decision, the options weighed and why one won. This skill writes one in a fixed template: status (proposed, accepted, deprecated or superseded), date and who signs off, then the context and the proposed decision. Each option considered gets a table rating complexity, cost, scalability and team familiarity, with pros and cons, followed by a trade-off analysis, the consequences (what gets easier, what gets harder, what to revisit) and a checklist of action items. It takes three kinds of request: a choice such as Kafka or SQS for an event bus, a review of an existing proposal, or the design of a new system, and points to the system-design skill for deeper frameworks. Stating constraints such as a deadline or a load target, naming the options and including latency, cost and maintenance needs sharpen its analysis. With a knowledge base connected it searches for earlier ADRs and design docs; with a project tracker it links related epics and tickets and creates implementation tasks.

Usage

Modes

Create an ADR: "Should we use Kafka or SQS for our event bus?" Evaluate a design: "Review this microservices proposal" System design: "Design the notification system for our app"

Tips

  1. State constraints upfront — "We need to ship in 2 weeks" or "Must handle 10K rps" shapes the answer.
  2. Name your options — Even if you're leaning one way, I'll give a more balanced analysis with explicit alternatives.
  3. Include non-functional requirements — Latency, cost, team expertise, and maintenance burden matter as much as features.
