cross-critique

Use it when
independent subagents produced divergent proposals on an architecture choice, a review disagreement or competing root-cause theories
It does
sends each author the other proposals, labelled neutrally, and asks for pros and cons of each, whether they'd revise their own, and a ranking with confidence
You get
a recommendation, how the critiques shifted things, why the winner survives them, and the remaining risks and unknowns
Needs
proposals from a first round, such as the council or research skill, and the same subagents to critique them
Skip it when
the proposals already agree, or the answer can be checked directly

TLDR

Synthesizing several subagents' reports alone makes the main agent the bottleneck: it only sees the tradeoffs it happens to notice. This skill has the authors poke holes in each other's reasoning instead. It assumes round one is done, with proposals made independently, by council, by parallel research subagents or ad hoc. The agent condenses each proposal to its recommendation and reasoning, labels them A, B, C and, where practical, hides who wrote which to reduce bias toward the most confident model. It then asks the same subagents, who keep their context, to weigh each rival with both pros and cons, say whether they'd change their own answer, and rank the options. A thin critique gets a follow-up to its author rather than being dropped. The synthesis weighs evidence rather than votes, treats agreement reached after critique as a strong signal, and surfaces the sharpest objection to each option. The round stays read-only unless the task involves changes.
