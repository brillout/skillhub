design-taste-frontend

Use it when
you're building or redesigning a landing page, marketing site or portfolio, or a page looks generic and AI-made
It does
has a script offer three design directions from a library built on 247 sites from galleries like Awwwards, then builds around an idea from your subject
You get
a finished page with full copy, real credited pictures and motion, plus a report listing the direction, critique scores and sample content to swap before launch
Checks
the page in a real browser at three widths, failing headlines over two lines, gradients, shadows, small labels above headings and empty stretches
Unlike the fixed looks
high-end-visual-design and minimalist-ui, the same repo's opt-in skills that each apply one style, it picks a new direction per project and measures the result
Needs
Node 22+, and Chrome, Edge or Chromium; network access to search open image collections

TLDR

A page an agent builds by default looks like the average of every page it has seen; this skill replaces those defaults with decisions. The agent first lists eight to twelve things only this client has, such as its street, its numbers or its rituals, and plans the page as claims with proof, one per section. A script picks three art directions at random from its library; the agent chooses the one that suits the subject, and the script then supplies its type, palette, hero and section layouts. The page needs an idea from the subject that a competitor couldn't use, and one interactive element built from it. The agent writes the full copy, then builds with real pictures: the project's own, generated ones, or credited images from open collections such as Wikimedia Commons and the Met. A linter and a browser inspector enforce its style rules, such as no gradients, monospace text, numbered steps or pill-shaped labels. The agent then critiques the screenshots, fixes and checks again, adds motion and reports. Dashboards and other app screens get their own process and rules; redesigns are measured before and after.

name: design-taste-frontend
description: >-
  tasteskill 2.5.0, the anti-slop frontend skill. Builds professional company websites at the level
  of figma.com and x.ai: plain copy, one system, one section per screen, equal edges, real
  photographs and real product UI (product renders when there are no photographs), with shader
  effects, scenes and moments only on tech, gaming and software pages, quiet micro-interactions,
  and a checker that enforces it in a real browser. Use this whenever you build, design, redesign,
  restyle, polish or review a landing page, company, product, shop, cafe, hotel, studio, SaaS, AI,
  developer-tool or game site, in any stack (HTML/CSS, React, Next.js, Vue, Svelte, Astro,
  Tailwind), or when the user says a page looks generic, templated or "AI-made".

tasteskill 2.5

Build the website a good company would ship: it says plainly what is sold, shows the real thing on every screen, and looks made for this client, not from a template and not like an art piece. figma.com and x.ai are the bar (in words: layout-v4.md section 1).

<skill> is this folder. It needs Node 22+ and Chrome, Edge or Chromium (ffmpeg optional). Copy <skill>/assets into the project's assets and start from assets/starter.html.

House rules

These nine win over everything else in this skill.

  1. No caption or description under any image, render or visual. Licence credits go in one footer line.
  2. Every visual renders, never an empty ground, at 1440, 1024, 768 and 390.
  3. One section per screen: the hero alone on the first screen, every section exactly one or two screens tall at 1440x900, no second section heading peeking in.
  4. Equal left and right content edges.
  5. Hover micro-animations: a linked card lifts 2px, a photo zooms 1.02-1.03 inside its frame, buttons step on hover and press on click, underlines draw, card loops cycle in 2.5-4 s, repeat and restart on hover. The motion layer does it; never strip or override it. No tilt, carousels or timed tabs.
  6. More and better real photographs where they fit; nicer backgrounds; better heroes, layouts and fonts.
  7. Real full-colour icons (a known product's real icon, never a drawn one); small diagram visuals in cards (a hub with branches; one input, a main processor, three parallel lanes; pulses running along the lines).
  8. The big award-site scenes and creative moments are part of the skill, but shader effects only where the trade has a technical subject (tech, AI, developer tools, gaming, fintech, hardware), never on a local cafe, bakery, guesthouse or shop: those get photographs.
  9. Addresses: street and district only (no street given: the district), no house number, postcode or coordinates; no register or VAT numbers; no phone in a real format; contacts on .example.

1. The kind of business decides everything

Write the kind on the plan's first line and declare it on the page: the page type on <html data-type> and <meta name="page-type"> (the same value), and <meta name="site-kind">; a place adds <meta name="page-kind" content="place">. The dealers take the last column (start.mjs --page, moments.mjs --page), the hero dealer the page type; consistency.mjs reads the metas.

Brief page type site-kind kind
App, SaaS, AI, developer tool, fintech or banking app, a studio selling its own software software software software
A game, a studio launching its game software gaming gaming
Hardware or a tech device (a drone, a robot arm, a keyboard) product tech tech
Any other physical product or brand (a chair, a bike, coffee beans sold online) product other product
People are hired: clinic, law firm, agency, creative studio, trade service other service
People come to it: cafe, bar, bakery, restaurant, guesthouse, small hotel, shop, florist, bike shop, gym, local studio place other place

A maker that also opens its door or teaches (a ceramics studio, a bakery with a web shop) is a place; its web shop becomes the goods section. It is a product only when nothing happens at the door.

"Tech" below means site-kind software, gaming or tech; "other" is everything else. Effects, scenes, moments, loops, counts, CSS grounds, dark pages and dark heroes are tech only. An other page is photographs, flat colour, type, real icons and the hover layer.

2. The page plan by kind

The full plans, deal lists and section order: references/sections.md.

  • Place (a cafe, bakery, guesthouse, shop). Photographs lead: 12 to 20 on the full page, 8 to 12 compact, under 8 frames the 3-screen short page, whatever the trade; no client photographs: the draft path (photos.md), then count. The hero a photograph whenever a frame fits (a remote place: its region's landscape), the visit block on screen two, the offer, the place, orders or booking. Variety without effects (sections.md section 4): the offer and the place deal different compositions, never two sections round one picture; one type moment (the address or name set large); one main action a screen; list text 16px. Flat grounds, square photographs, one button style, the phone bar; no effect, ground, moment, loop, fragment, diagram or tab.
  • Product and service (site-kind other). Photographs lead: the product, the people, the rooms, the work, two or more a screen on a product page. Real icons for the tools a studio uses; one still diagram at most; flat grounds and one brand band. No shader effect, print, grain, gradient, moment, scene or loop; WebGL only for the product render. A physical product with no photographs is shown: its render (assets/objects/product-render, stills) leads the hero, then the full product page, never a drawing or mood stock (sections.md).
  • Every product page (photographed or rendered, hardware too; the bar is Figure and 1X; sections.md, Product): the product big where it stands whole (most of its frame, or on the ground at anchor scale), never mid-size in a grey rounded panel on a grey ground; a different picture each section, never one view twice; every headline promise pictured (a family bike shows the child bench); dealt compositions, no structure twice, one contrasting ground; the swatch changes the picture, every colour shown once; no fact twice; buttons step and press (rule 5).
  • Tech (software, gaming, hardware). The client's own subject leads at true size. Software: its own UI as live HTML showing what it works on (a code-review tool: a red and green diff with its Accept action) in one app-frame, never a generic dashboard or only fragments on panels; cards: fragments on lit panels, pulsing diagrams, real icons. Hardware: its photographs, else its render (as above), never a split hero of only an order form or a drawn SVG. A game: its name in the h1, a launch line, its world as full-bleed key art (else its render), an effect never in its place (sections.md, Gaming). 60 to 80 words a screen outside UI mocks (copy.md). By deal: one effect (a shader ground in the hero or the one [data-visual] frame, a WebGL part, an object or one big scene) and one or two moments in their own sections; four live WebGL contexts at most; no card moves with the scroll (effects-usage.md).
  • One object never carries the page. The product or hero object whole leads the hero and two more sections at most; another kind of visual the brief supports carries the rest (sections.md section 1, its table): a game's world, crew and map; hardware's use at the bench, an exploded or parts view; a still diagram card, a type moment, a context photograph where the trade allows.
  • The client's own assets get slots. A trailer, key art, screenshots, CAD renders or a motion video the brief names each get a frame that reads finished now (a real <video> with a kit poster, a play control only when there is a page or file to play, three to five world captures from the kit, a CAD frame), named in a comment for the client's file, never empty (sections-library.md, client-slots).

4. The run

  1. Read the brief. Write down the kind (section 1), what is sold, who buys it, the one main action (the transaction: Book a room, Order a cake, Start a free trial; a place with neither: Get directions) and the real material: logo, screens, photographs, prices, address, hours.

  2. Write all the copy first, as copy.md says: the h1 names the category and the buyer or the place (a game: its name and genre), one hard fact on the first screen, a noun in every heading, buttons as a verb and an object. Nothing is invented but a coined brand name (searched first, the result kept in names.json beside the page) and, on a place, plausible hours and prices.

  3. Plan the page as screens. Line 1: the kind. Line 2: the deal (step 4). Then one line per section: its id, its one idea, its picture and its kind (object, use, parts, world, slot, diagram, type; three object lines at most), its screens (1 or 2, from its content height, layout-v4.md section 2), and on tech which section holds the effect, the scene or a moment. Pick the photographs now (photos.md); their count, stand-ins included, sets a place's length (section 2). Then read cards.md and the recipe of every id you use in sections-library.md.

  4. Deal, never choose by taste. Each command keeps a history, so pages differ:

    node <skill>/assets/layout/deal.mjs --type <page type> --has <photo,ui,object,film> --history .taste-skill/layout-history.json   # the hero; a game adds --kind gaming, hardware --kind tech
node <skill>/scripts/start.mjs deal --page <kind> --history .taste-skill/history.json    # three palettes, three pairings; add --axis after a word or editorial hero
node <skill>/scripts/start.mjs css --page <kind> --palette <id> --type <id> --radius soft --history .taste-skill/history.json
node <skill>/scripts/start.mjs brand --page place --bg "#.." --ink "#.." --accent "#.." [--surface "#.."] [--muted "#.."] --type <id> --radius soft   # a place: its own colours, in place of css
node -e "for(const l of process.argv.slice(1)){const o=l.split('|');console.log(o[Math.floor(Math.random()*o.length)])}" <the kind's lists in sections.md>

    A product, hardware or a game with no photographs or art deals with --has object: always the product hero, its render's widest view across the wrap, no box (a game always passes --has). A place's hero is printed, never dealt (--type place). A dealt part that needs a photograph the page lacks is dealt again (sections.md section 2). Take the palette and pairing that suit the client (its own colours: start.mjs brand, always on a place, with sections.md section 4, Look); never violet: deal again. Keep the :root block as printed. Radius: sharp for fintech and developer tools, soft by default, round for consumer apps, square on a place. On tech only: the lit ground (<html data-ground>: sand|sky|sage through the one-liner), the CSS grounds (backgrounds.md), the one effect (effects-usage.md) and the moments (moments.md). Write every result on plan line 2 and in the comment at the top of <body>, and build exactly that (a short page drops what it has no section for: sections.md section 4).

  5. Build. In starter.html: set <html data-type data-radius data-buttons data-motion> and the three metas, the nav class; copy only the <section class="hero …"> of assets/layout/hero-<id>.html (never its nav, other sections, footer or credit line; every word and picture becomes the client's); move the dealt close and footer out of their <template>s and delete the rest. Every section is main > section with one .wrap, its h2 first, --h: 1 or 2. Build each to its recipe with the system's classes and tokens; write page CSS only where a reference says page CSS (inside a visual, --p-h, .wall, .rail, .device). Paste UI from assets/ui/<name>/fragment.html and rewrite every name, number and logo to the client's (kit icons become data-icon, cards.md); a diagram card is data-diagram="<preset>" with the client's labels; run icons.mjs scan index.html --out assets/icons --colour last, so every icon the page names is a local file. Mount an effect, object, moment or scene exactly as its reference says, never from your own script. Credits: the one footer line from photos.mjs credits, once the markup uses the photographs (it reads the page).

  6. Check and look. Only the skill's scripts, each running one headless Chrome with WebGL on the CPU, closed by its own PID, one at a time; never a windowed browser or a browser tool. Each serves the page file itself over http on 127.0.0.1 (never file://), so no server is needed; for a built page whose links start at the site root (/_astro/page.css), pass --root with the build folder (dist). Then:

    node <skill>/scripts/consistency.mjs index.html --widths 1440,1024,820,768,390 --rhythm-widths 1440,820,768,390 --profile <dir> --page <kind> --category "<what is sold>" --primary "<main action>"
node <skill>/assets/layout/check.mjs --url index.html --profile <dir> --sizes 1440x900,1024x768,820x1180,768x1024,390x844 --shots shots/layout
node <skill>/scripts/shoot.mjs --url index.html --w 1440 --out shots/1440.png --profile <dir>
node <skill>/scripts/perf.mjs index.html --profile <dir>

    Shoot 1024, 820x1180, 768x1024 and 390 as well. Every check.mjs finding fails the page (no --no-stress). Fix every FAIL (one you judge false goes in the report with its reason), shoot again, and read every screenshot against the checklist.

  7. Report: the kind, the deal (palette, pairing, hero, grounds, effect, moments, scene); the checker, layout and perf results; what you could not check; every assumed price, hour or address and the coined name's search; the photographs and art the client must supply, every stand-in frame among them; every client slot, its file and its stand-in.

5. Checklist

Each line names its checks: consistency.mjs ids in brackets, check.mjs findings in braces, perf.mjs rows. The last line is for your eyes.

  • First screen: says what is sold, for whom or where, with one hard fact [copy-headline, copy-fact]; the hero fills it alone at every width, its main button inside it [hero-screen, hero-fill, fold-action, fold-cut] {hero-fold}.
  • One section per screen: every section 1 or 2 screens, its h2 first and top-aligned, no neighbour's heading or picture in its tile, nothing sparse [section-height, one-section, one-heading, section-air, one-screen, section-gaps] {screen-short, screen-height, sparse, neighbour, neighbour-ground}.
  • Equal edges: every block on the .wrap lines or the grid's columns, nothing out to one side, no sideways scroll [container-edges, row-lines, split-pair, off-grid, right-edge, col-edge, spill, off-viewport, stagger, top-stagger] {edge-left, edge-right, spill, bleed-one-side, one-sided, lines}.
  • Every visual draws at 1440, 1024, 768 and 390; a refused effect shows its still fallback [visual-empty, empty-visual, gl-fallback, shader-lint, no-visual, photo-weight].
  • No caption explains, apologises for or labels a picture; credits in one footer line [visual-caption, caption, credit-wall, photo-credit]; no placeholder, sample note or printed .example address [placeholder, sample-label].
  • The right things for the kind: no effect ground, print, scene or moment outside tech (WebGL only for a product render) [effect-kind, effects-fit, photo-print]; the subject itself shows: the product big, whole in three sections at most, a new picture each [product-shown, same-object, photo-repeat]; each asset the brief names in its slot; a game's name and its world as key art, the diff of a code-review tool, software's app-frame; real photographs where they fit, none twice [photo-set, photo-repeat, photo-upscale]; real icons in their own colours; flat grounds on other [grounds]; a place deals two different compositions and one type moment.
  • Motion: every button, link, linked card and linked photograph visibly answers the pointer, nothing tilts [motion-layer, hover, hover-move, tilt]; card loops cycle in 2.5-4 s [loop-cycle]; nothing moves because the page scrolled except a dealt moment or the big scene inside its own section on tech [scroll-linked, scroll-reveal, pinned: expected only there]; anything moving past 5 s has a pause button [loop-pause]; under reduced motion everything rests and nothing stays hidden [reduced-motion, hidden-content]; at most four live contexts [webgl].
  • Contacts as rule 9 [street-number, postcode, coordinates, company-number, phone-number].
  • One system: one button pair, one card style, one radius scale, one link look [button-styles, button-shape, card-family, card-layout, link-styles]; two boxes deep at most [nesting]; one heading scale, its faces loaded [heading-scale, font-load]; text contrast [text-contrast, contrast].
  • Copy and links: headings name things, buttons a verb and an object, at most two hero actions [copy-headings, copy-buttons, copy-actions, action-count]; one fact, one wording [fact-agree]; every link lands, forms send [links, forms].
  • Fast: no FAIL row in perf.mjs (image weight, contexts, layout shift).
  • 820, 768 and 390: sections on their screens [section-height], the first screen ending whole or on a clear bleed [fold-card] (layout-v4.md); splits stack text first, no row half empty; one column at 390, buttons full width, a full-screen menu; the place action bar.
  • Your eyes: beside the figma.com and x.ai frames it reads as a professional company site; nothing tilted, staggered, cut or overlapping; the hero is the work, not a mood.

6. Never

  • A card, row or tile that animates on scroll; a reveal, fade-in or parallax; random blinking.
  • Stock photographs passed off as the client's product, people or place; a product page that never shows the product.
  • A drawn look-alike of a real logo; invented reviews, quotes, customers or figures.
  • Violet or lavender in the page's own design: accent, ground, gradient, effect colour or tint (a real third-party logo keeps its own colours, icons.md).
  • Copying a reference site's code, copy, pictures or signature effect.
  • Deleting or editing the kit's own files (assets/, scripts/) to clear a check: a check that flags a kit file is reported, and the page is checked again after any change.
