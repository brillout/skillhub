TLDR

Guidance from Resend for email that gets delivered, stays legal and is easy to read. A quick-reference table points the agent to eleven reference files: deliverability (SPF, DKIM, DMARC and spam fixes), transactional emails such as password resets and one-time codes, a catalog of the emails an app typically needs, email capture and validation, newsletters and promotions, CAN-SPAM, GDPR and CASL compliance, telling transactional from marketing email, retries and idempotent sending, delivery webhooks, bounce and complaint handling with suppression lists, and accessibility (lang and dir, presentational tables, headings, alt text, title and contrast). It also lays out paths through them: for spam problems, check authentication first, since Gmail and Yahoo reject unauthenticated mail; for marketing email, collect consent, check the legal requirements, then send; for production sending, add retries and idempotency, then webhooks to track delivery, then bounce handling.

Email Best Practices

Guidance for building deliverable, compliant, user-friendly emails.

Architecture Overview

Start Here

New app? Start with the Catalog to plan which emails your app needs (password reset, verification, etc.), then set up Deliverability (DNS authentication) before sending your first email.

Spam issues? Check Deliverability first—authentication problems are the most common cause. Gmail/Yahoo reject unauthenticated emails.

Marketing emails? Follow this path: Email Capture (collect consent) → Compliance (legal requirements) → Marketing Emails (best practices).

Production-ready sending? Add reliability: Sending Reliability (retry + idempotency) → Webhooks & Events (track delivery) → List Management (handle bounces).

Accessibility? Most emails fail basic accessibility checks. See Accessibility for lang/dir, presentational tables, headings, alt text, <title>, and contrast.
