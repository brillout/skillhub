TLDR

From Claude Office Skills. You describe the opportunity, your solution and pricing, and the type and length of proposal you need, and the agent fills one of three templates. The full sales or service proposal covers an executive summary that can stand alone, the client's situation, goals and requirements, the proposed solution with deliverables and phases, priced options with one marked as recommended, what is and isn't included, a timeline, case studies and team, next steps and terms. A quick quote has the scope, a line-item price table with tax, dates, terms and a signature line; a partnership proposal sets out the value for each side, roles, revenue sharing and a pilot with KPIs. Its writing advice: show you understand the client in their own terms, focus on outcomes rather than activities, anchor pricing with the higher option first, and remove friction from saying yes. You can ask for brief, standard or comprehensive length, a conservative, modern or technical style, and a document, slides or email-friendly format, all written as Markdown: the Word and PowerPoint tools its frontmatter names are never called. It notes that legal terms should be reviewed by counsel.

Proposal Writer

Overview

This skill helps you create professional, persuasive business proposals that clearly communicate value and win deals. From simple quotes to comprehensive RFP responses.

Use Cases:

  • Sales proposals and quotes
  • Project proposals
  • Partnership proposals
  • RFP/RFI responses
  • Grant applications
  • Service agreements

How to Use

  1. Tell me about the opportunity (client, project, requirements)
  2. Share your solution and pricing
  3. Specify the proposal type and length needed
  4. I'll create a structured, compelling proposal

Example prompts:

  • "Create a proposal for website redesign services"
  • "Write a partnership proposal for joint marketing"
  • "Draft an RFP response for IT services"
  • "Generate a project proposal for the new feature"

Proposal Writing Best Practices

Executive Summary
  • Write it last (after full proposal)
  • Make it stand alone
  • Lead with client benefits, not your features
  • Keep to 1 page max
Understanding Section
  • Show you've done your homework
  • Reference specific client challenges
  • Use their language and terminology
  • Acknowledge their goals
Solution Section
  • Focus on outcomes, not activities
  • Explain WHY, not just WHAT
  • Use visuals where helpful
  • Make it easy to understand
Pricing Section
  • Offer options when possible
  • Anchor with higher option first
  • Be transparent about what's included
  • Justify the investment
Call to Action
  • Make next steps crystal clear
  • Remove friction (easy to say yes)
  • Create urgency appropriately
  • Provide contact for questions

  • Style: Conservative/corporate, modern/creative, technical
  • Focus: Price-competitive, value-focused, relationship-building
  • Format: Document, presentation slides, email-friendly

Limitations

  • Cannot research the specific client (provide context)
  • Pricing must be provided by you
  • Legal terms should be reviewed by counsel
  • Industry-specific regulations may apply
  • Cannot guarantee deal closure
