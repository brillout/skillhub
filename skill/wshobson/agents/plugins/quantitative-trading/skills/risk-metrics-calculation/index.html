TLDR

A reference for portfolio risk measurement, not a workflow. The SKILL.md groups metrics into volatility, tail risk, drawdown and risk-adjusted categories and sets out do's and don'ts: use several metrics, don't lean on VaR alone, assume fat tails rather than normality, watch correlation in stress, and use long lookbacks. A reference file holds the Python: a RiskMetrics class (volatility, downside deviation, beta, three VaR methods, CVaR, drawdown series and durations, Sharpe, Sortino, Calmar, Omega, information ratio and a summary), a PortfolioRisk class (portfolio volatility, marginal and component risk contributions, risk-parity weights, diversification ratio, tracking error), rolling versions over a window, and a StressTester with named historical crises (2008, 2020 COVID, 2022 rate hikes, dot-com, flash crash), hypothetical shocks and Monte Carlo at elevated volatility.

Risk Metrics Calculation

Comprehensive risk measurement toolkit for portfolio management, including Value at Risk, Expected Shortfall, and drawdown analysis.

When to Use This Skill

  • Measuring portfolio risk
  • Implementing risk limits
  • Building risk dashboards
  • Calculating risk-adjusted returns
  • Setting position sizes
  • Regulatory reporting

Core Concepts

1. Risk Metric Categories
Category Metrics Use Case
Volatility Std Dev, Beta General risk
Tail Risk VaR, CVaR Extreme losses
Drawdown Max DD, Calmar Capital preservation
Risk-Adjusted Sharpe, Sortino Performance

Best Practices

Do's
  • Use multiple metrics - No single metric captures all risk
  • Consider tail risk - VaR isn't enough, use CVaR
  • Rolling analysis - Risk changes over time
  • Stress test - Historical and hypothetical
  • Document assumptions - Distribution, lookback, etc.
Don'ts
  • Don't rely on VaR alone - Underestimates tail risk
  • Don't assume normality - Returns are fat-tailed
  • Don't ignore correlation - Increases in stress
  • Don't use short lookbacks - Miss regime changes
  • Don't forget transaction costs - Affects realized risk
